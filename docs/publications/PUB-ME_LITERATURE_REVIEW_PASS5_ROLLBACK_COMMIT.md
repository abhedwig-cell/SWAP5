# PUB-ME literature review pass 5: rollback, commit and irreversible side effects

Status: **ADVERSARIAL_PRIOR_ART__MECHANISM_NOVELTY_REMOVED__MODERNIZATION_APPLICATION_GAP_REMAINS_OPEN**

Publication owner: `PUB-ME`

Doctoral mapping: `RQ1 / PRESERVE`

Review date: 2026-09-18

## 1. Review question

After D1-D6, the strongest empirical pattern is not unique fault detection but either:

- structural prevention at the candidate-to-accepted boundary; or
- earlier detection before rejected/speculative work becomes accepted scientific history.

This pass therefore asks:

> Are rollback-safe state authority, commit semantics and accepted-only external side effects already established simulation concepts, and has that same logic already been evaluated as a modernization method for mature process-based scientific models?

## 2. Strong prior art: optimistic simulation / Time Warp

### Jefferson et al. (1987), Time Warp Operating System

The Time Warp Operating System implements optimistic discrete-event simulation using general distributed rollback and explicitly treats commitment as a core part of the execution model.

Consequence for PUB-ME:

- speculative versus committed simulation history is not new;
- rollback and commit are not new;
- PUB-ME must not claim invention of an accepted-state lifecycle.

### Lin and Lazowska (1991), A Study of Time Warp Rollback Mechanisms

Peer-reviewed Time Warp work studies rollback and cancellation behavior, including how outputs/messages generated by speculative events must be handled after rollback.

Consequence:

- undoing effects of invalid speculative execution is mature simulation research.

### Schordan et al. (2020), Reversible Languages and Incremental State Saving in Optimistic PDES

The reviewed chapter explicitly describes event executions as speculative/provisional, restoration of prior state after causality conflict, cancellation of effects, re-execution, and eventual commit once rollback is no longer possible.

Consequence:

- candidate/provisional state, checkpoint/restore, rollback, re-execution and commit are established concepts.

### Cingolani, Pellegrini and Quaglia (2017), state recovery in optimistic PDES

Rollback/state recovery using checkpointing, undo logs and reverse computation has been studied extensively for correctness/performance.

Consequence:

- state recovery after speculative computation is established prior art.

## 3. Direct prior art for D6: output commit

Antonacci, Pellegrini and Quaglia (2013), *Consistent and efficient output-streams management in optimistic simulation platforms*, DOI 10.1145/2486092.2486133, is especially important.

The paper treats interaction with the outside world as a known rollback problem because external output may not itself be reversible. Its output subsystem ensures that produced output is consistent with events that are eventually committed.

This is directly analogous at the mechanism level to D6.

Consequence:

- accepted-only external publication is not novel;
- "irreversible side effects must wait for commitment" is not a PUB-ME invention;
- D6 is useful empirical evidence for modernization qualification, not a new simulation principle.

## 4. Transactional simulation prior art

Hoverd and Sampson (2010), *A Transactional Architecture for Simulation*, DOI 10.1109/ICECCS.2010.7, explicitly transfers transaction concepts into simulation design.

Consequence:

- the word `transactional` cannot carry PUB-ME novelty;
- atomic/consistent state update ideas are established outside hydrology.

## 5. Earth-system reproducibility prior art remains important

Mahajan et al. (2019), *Ongoing solution reproducibility of earth system models as they progress toward exascale computing*, DOI 10.1177/1094342019837341, already provides a methodology for establishing solution reproducibility during ongoing software-infrastructure evolution when no science change is intended.

Consequence:

- continuous preservation during development is not novel;
- PUB-ME cannot claim novelty merely because equivalence is rechecked across evolving software.

## 6. Emerging modernization validation

Coleman et al. (2026), *Validating LLM-Modernized Scientific Software Through Differential Fault Injection*, arXiv:2608.14527, is currently a preprint.

It prospectively applies identical off-nominal faults to legacy and modernized scientific software and compares their responses.

Consequence:

- adversarial/fault-injection validation of scientific modernization is now emerging prior art;
- PUB-ME cannot rely on fault injection itself as novelty.

## 7. What this pass removes from the novelty claim

PUB-ME must not claim novelty for:

- speculative versus committed state;
- rollback or retry;
- state checkpointing/restoration;
- transaction semantics in simulation;
- accepted-only irreversible output;
- rollback-safe message/output management;
- ongoing solution reproducibility during software changes;
- differential/fault-injection validation of modernization.

These mechanisms are prior art.

## 8. Remaining candidate distinction

This pass did **not** identify a close peer-reviewed study that combines all of the following as a prospective empirical modernization study of a mature process-based scientific model:

1. a frozen legacy scientific denominator;
2. a strong conventional scientific-software comparator, not an output-only straw man;
3. preregistered semantic contamination families spanning physical state, accounting, persistence, numerical workspace and external publication;
4. explicit candidate-to-accepted authority oracles;
5. comparative measurement of whether those oracles structurally prevent or detect contamination earlier than the strong comparator;
6. representative preservation evidence across staged architectural migration;
7. later evidence that solver/coupling extensions reuse the same preserved scientific denominator.

This is a bounded search result, not a universal priority proof.

## 9. Revised novelty wording

Do not write:

> We introduce transactional scientific model execution that distinguishes speculative and committed state.

That would be false as a novelty claim.

A defensible bounded formulation is closer to:

> We evaluate whether explicit transition-authority contracts, adapted from established transaction/rollback concepts, provide measurable qualification value when a mature time-stepped scientific model is architecturally modernized while preserving a declared scientific reference.

Even this wording remains provisional until physical-regime replication and final literature closure.

## 10. Implication for D1-D6

The D1-D6 results should be interpreted as an **application/evaluation study of established authority concepts in scientific-model modernization**, not as six new mechanisms.

The main empirical result is currently:

- no UNIQUE_DETECTION;
- two STRUCTURAL_PREVENTION results;
- four EARLIER_DETECTION results;
- the earlier-detection cases span accounting, restart/persistence, solver-workspace misuse and external publication.

This supports a temporal/localization contribution, subject to replication.

## 11. Remaining falsification target

The standalone paper should be abandoned or reframed if later literature identifies a peer-reviewed modernization study that already:

- uses a comparably strong B1 baseline;
- prospectively seeds state/history contamination faults;
- evaluates candidate-to-accepted authority across multiple scientific-state side channels;
- and demonstrates the same earlier-detection/structural-prevention value during migration of a mature scientific simulator.
